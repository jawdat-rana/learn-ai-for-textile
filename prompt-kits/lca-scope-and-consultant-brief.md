# Kit 3: LCA scope and consultant brief

**Use it when:** you're about to ask consultants to quote for an LCA, or
you want to set up an in-house screening properly. A clear goal-and-scope
brief makes quotes faster, cheaper and comparable, and stops you paying for
a study you didn't need.

**Try it with:** the P01 Performance T-shirt from
[Module 1](../module-1-product-lca/README.md). An example result is in
[module-1-product-lca/output/consultant_brief.md](../module-1-product-lca/output/consultant_brief.md).

## Step 1: Let the AI interview you

```text
You are a senior LCA consultant helping a textile technology manager write a
goal-and-scope brief following the structure of ISO 14044. Interview me,
one question at a time, at most 12 questions, about:
- why we want the study and who will see the results (internal, customers,
  public; comparative or not)
- the product, its functional unit and its expected life
- the system boundary and anything we want excluded
- which impact categories matter to us, and why
- what data we can provide and by when
- budget and timeline
After each answer, tell me in one line if my answer changes the cost or
rigour needed (for example, a public comparative claim needs a critical
review panel).
```

## Step 2: Write the brief

```text
Now write the brief (under 2 pages): goal and audience, product and
functional unit, system boundary (in and out), impact categories, data we
provide, what we want delivered, timeline, and 8 questions to ask every
consultant so quotes are comparable. Mark anything still open with
[DECISION]. Then list 3 ways to cut the cost of this study without making it
less useful.
```

## What good looks like

The brief makes the **intended use** explicit (internal or public, comparative
or not), because that drives cost the most. It asks consultants which
database and version they'll use, how they handle recycled content and
allocation, whether critical review is included, and who owns the model.
Cost-cutting ideas include screening first to focus primary data collection,
limiting the number of products and options, and reusing the model for
future products.

## Where AI is wrong

- **It over-scopes.** Left alone, it'll ask for every impact category and
  every product. Ask: "What's the smallest study that answers our question?"
- **It mixes up standards.** ISO 14040/44, ISO 14067, the GHG Protocol
  Product Standard and EU PEF have different rules. Ask it which one it's
  applying and why.
- **It guesses prices.** Don't trust AI estimates of consultancy fees.
  Get quotes.

## Make it yours

Save your finished brief as a template. Next time, paste it and ask the AI
to adapt it for a new product.
