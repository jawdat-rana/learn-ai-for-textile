# Screening carbon footprint: six products (internal)

*Example output from Prompt 8. Made-up products. Screening level only.*

**Headline.** Our six products range from 1.7 to 12.8 kg CO2e per garment
across their life. Dyeing and finishing is the biggest stage for most
polyester products, flying the leggings makes transport their biggest
stage, and washing and drying dominate the workwear trousers. We recommend
measuring dye-house energy first and moving the leggings to sea freight.

| Product | kg CO2e per garment | Main hotspot |
|---|---|---|
| P01 Performance T-shirt | 2.4 | Dyeing and finishing (29%) |
| P02 Classic cotton T-shirt | 3.1 | Yarn spinning (25%) |
| P03 Fleece jacket | 6.6 | Dyeing and finishing (33%) |
| P04 Sports leggings | 7.9 | Air freight (34%) |
| P05 Workwear trousers | 12.8 | Use: washing and drying (42%) |
| P06 Swim shorts | 1.7 | Dyeing and finishing (28%) |

**Three levers to test first**

1. **Ship P04 by sea:** -33% for that product. Needs a planning change, not a
   material change.
2. **Dye-house energy:** moving P01's dyeing and finishing from a
   coal-steam mill in China to a gas mill on a cleaner grid (Portugal) cut it
   by 27%. Before moving anything, ask our current mills about heat recovery
   and fuel switching.
3. **Recycled polyester for P01:** -12%. Smaller than expected, because most
   of the footprint is in processing, not the fibre.

**Data to collect next**

- Dye houses for P01 and P03: kWh and MJ of steam per kg of fabric, and the
  boiler fuel. The dyeing heat factor alone moves P01 between 2.1 and 3.0.
- Polyester fibre supplier: a product-specific footprint (cradle to gate).
- P06 supplier: the missing 15% (lining fibre and recycled content).

> **Limits of this screening.** Not an ISO 14040/44 study. Climate change
> only (no water, toxicity or microplastics). Excludes chemicals and dyes,
> water treatment, detergent, retail and upstream fuel supply, so results
> are probably a little low. Factors are rounded public averages with wide
> ranges. **These numbers must not be used in marketing, on labels or in
> customer documents.**
