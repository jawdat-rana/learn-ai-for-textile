# Fibre options for the performance T-shirt: recommendation

*Example output from Prompt 7. Made-up product and costs. Screening level
only.*

**Recommendation: trial recycled polyester (option B) first.** Per wear it is
the lowest-carbon option in this screening (43 g CO2e, range 41-45, versus 49 g
for virgin polyester), and its range does not overlap with virgin
polyester's. Fibre cost rises from about €0.23 to €0.29 per garment.

**What we can and can't conclude**

- Recycled polyester is likely lower-carbon than virgin polyester per wear.
- We **can't** separate virgin polyester from the "biodegradable additive"
  option (E), or organic from conventional cotton, on climate: their ranges
  overlap.
- The cotton options look worse per wear mainly because we assumed fewer
  washes (40 vs 50). Organic cotton would need about 67 washes to match virgin
  polyester. We have no durability data to support either number.
- None of the polyester options reduce persistent microfibres. Option E
  claims to, but we have no test evidence.

**Evidence needed before deciding**

1. Supplier cradle-to-gate footprints for virgin and recycled fibre, with
   method, database and allocation stated.
2. Our own wash-durability tests for each candidate fabric.
3. For option E: biodegradation test reports for the relevant environments,
   ecotoxicity of residues, and confirmation that it doesn't harm polyester
   recycling.

> **Do not claim**
> - "Our T-shirt is carbon neutral / low carbon": a screening estimate can't
>   support it, and EU rules restrict generic claims.
> - "X% lower emissions than before": needs a verified comparative study.
> - "Biodegradable" or "ocean safe": needs test evidence for the specific
>   environment and conditions, stated next to the claim.
> - "Recycled polyester is better for the oceans": it sheds microfibres like
>   virgin polyester.
